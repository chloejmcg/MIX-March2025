Hatchability

On day 21 (embryonic), the Gallus gallus embryos hatch into prococial chicks. For this study, there were 6 groups of 26 eggs. The groups were

  1. Non injection
  2. Sterile MilliQ water
  3. Sterile 0.85% NaCl MilliQ water
  4. MIX Dose 1 (lowest)
  5. MIX Dose 2
  6. MIX Dose 3 (highest)

The raw hatch data was as follows;

  1. 23 out of 26 (88.46%)
  2. 7 out of 26 (26.92%)
  3. 0 out of 26 (0%)
  4. 24 out of 26 (92.31%)
  5. 26 out of 26 (100%)
  6. 24 out of 26 (92.31%)

Based on the raw hatch data, there are clear differences in hatchability rates among the groups. I hypothesize that the low temperature of water stored in the freezer prior to injection shocked the eggs and therefore decreased hatchability. Additionally, the saline still had ice in it, so the hypothesis is that the salt precipitated out of the frozen water, making the salinity higher than 0.85% and enough to kill the embryos.

The anova_body p value is 0.395, therefore no significant difference in body weight at hatch. However, I still wanted to run a Tukey pair-wise comparison to see if there was something further able to detect statistically. The tukey_body results confirmed that there are no significant differences between groups.

The p-value for the anova_ceca is 0.118. This means there are no significant differences in the ceca weight at hatch. This was expected because 1) a disease model was not introduced to cause a significant effect on the ceca weight and 2) the sensitivity of the scale at the farm is limited so that may inhib any difference at the milligram level to be detected.

Likewise for the tukey_ceca analyis, there was no significant difference as the pairwise level for the treatments and the groups.
